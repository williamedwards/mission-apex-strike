# Building chambers in the Unity editor

Chambers are ordinary Unity scenes that you edit by hand, saved in `Assets/_Project/Levels/`.
They're separate from `CameraTestBed`, which is generated by code and gets overwritten on every
rebuild, so don't build real levels there.

Sizes and rules to build to are in [LEVEL_METRICS.md](LEVEL_METRICS.md). The short version:
- **Grid:** build on a 1 m grid.
- **Quarter-pipes:** 2.5 m radius.
- **Changing orientation:** the boots only do it along curves; a sharp join is impassable.
- **What's magnetic:** only the bright bevelled panels.

## 1. Start a chamber
1. Menu **GravBoots → Level Kit** opens the kit window. Dock it somewhere handy by dragging its
   tab, for example next to the Inspector.
2. Click **New Chamber…** and give it a name, such as `Chamber_01`. You get a playable starter
   scene: a magnetic floor, a wall, an example quarter-pipe, a Start spawn point, and the
   player, camera and HUD already set up.
3. Press **Play** (the ▶ at the top of the editor) to try it straight away. Press it again to stop.
   **Anything you change while playing is thrown away when you stop**, so edit levels only
   while stopped.

## 2. Getting around the Scene view
| Do this | How |
|---|---|
| Fly around | Hold **right mouse** and use **W A S D**, plus **Q / E** for down and up. Hold **Shift** to go faster. |
| Orbit around what you're looking at | **Alt + left mouse** drag |
| Pan | **Middle mouse** drag |
| Zoom | Scroll wheel |
| Jump to the selection | Select something (click it, or click its name in the Hierarchy), then press **F** |
| Look straight down an axis | Click the cone arms of the gizmo in the top-right corner of the Scene view. Click the centre cube to switch between perspective and flat (isometric). |

Tip: Top and side views, with the flat view on, make lining blocks up much easier.

## 3. Placing and shaping pieces
- **Adding pieces:** the kit's **Add** buttons put a piece at the centre of the Scene view,
  snapped to the grid, and select it. To control where pieces go, fly to roughly the right spot
  first.
- **Moving:** press **W** (Move tool) and drag the arrows. Moves snap to the kit's grid step
  (0.5 m by default).
- **Resizing a block:** with a block selected, drag the small **dots on its faces**. Each dot
  moves that face only, in grid steps, and the opposite face stays put. (Blocks also
  convert the Scale tool into proper resizes, but the dots are easier.)
- **Rotating:** use the kit's **Turn / Tilt / Roll 90°** buttons, or **E** (Rotate tool), which
  snaps to 90°.
- **Duplicating:** **Ctrl+D**, then move the copy. This is the fastest way to build walls and
  repeated features.
- **Undo:** **Ctrl+Z** undoes anything. **Ctrl+S** saves the chamber; do it often.

### Surfaces
Select pieces and use **Magnetic / Plain / Lethal** in the kit. Magnetic pieces are the bright
bevelled panels the boots stick to. Lethal pieces are orange; the landing marker shows them in
red, and from Phase 2 they'll kill.

### Curves
- **Quarter-pipe:** an inside corner, like the example in a new chamber.
- **Rounded edge:** an outside corner, for going over the top of a block.

A new curve sits at the spot where it starts, on a surface, and bends in the direction of its
blue (forward) arrow:
- A quarter-pipe bends **up**: from a floor into a wall ahead.
- A rounded edge bends **down**: over an edge ahead.

Turn it with the 90° buttons until it faces the right way, then move it so it meets the surfaces
at both ends flush. You set **Radius** and **Width** in the Inspector; keep quarter-pipes at
2.5 m unless you're experimenting.

Note the wall meets the floor along the quarter-pipe's far edge, so a wall has to sit exactly one
radius away from where the curve starts.

### Spawn points
The **Spawn Point** button adds a numbered spawn. The player starts at **Start**, and **N** cycles
through the spawns in order while playing. That's handy for testing the far end of a chamber.

### Death, checkpoints and the exit
- **Kill Volume:** an invisible box (red outline in the Scene view) that kills on entry. Fill
  the bottom of pits with one so falls end quickly. Resize it with its face dots like a block.
  Orange **Lethal** surfaces kill on touch too. Falling far outside the chamber kills anyway,
  but slowly.
- **Checkpoint:** a pad that lights up when walked through. Dying puts you back on the most
  recent one. Turn it onto a wall or ceiling with the 90° buttons and you respawn there with
  the boots on (it must sit on a magnetic surface). The green box is its trigger; resize it in
  the Inspector (Box Collider → Size) to cover the whole route past it.
- **Exit:** the gold pad that finishes the chamber.
- A chamber made before these existed needs **Add Game Loop to This Chamber** (top of the kit)
  once. One made before the playtest log shows **Add Playtest Log to This Chamber** instead.

### Sentries
- **Sentry:** a robot that sees and shoots only in one flat plane, 1 m above the surface it
  stands on.
- Its searchlight sweeps round. If you're in the plane, inside the searchlight and in its line
  of sight, it locks on, charges, then fires. One hit kills.
- In the last fifth of a second of the charge the light turns white and stops aiming: step
  aside or leave the plane.
- The red line traced round the room is where its plane meets surfaces. The same line shows in
  the Scene view while you edit, so you can see what it covers.
- **Counterplay is changing surface.** Climb a wall above the line, or walk the ceiling over it.
- **Dropping onto its head destroys it for good.** You need to land on it from a drop of at
  least half a metre; walking onto it doesn't count.
  - Drops fall toward the world floor, so only floor sentries can be stomped.
  - Sentries on walls and ceilings can only be avoided.
- In the Inspector:
  - **Range** (14 m): how far its plane reaches.
  - **Sweep Degrees Per Second**: 0 makes a stationary guard.
  - **Sweep Arc**: under 360 makes it sweep back and forth around its forward (blue arrow).
  - **View Half Angle**: how wide the searchlight is.

### Turrets (placeholder, likely to be removed)
- **Turret:** a laser turret that sees the side its base faces. Rotate it onto a wall or ceiling
  with the 90° buttons. Solid geometry blocks its line of sight.
- It tracks with a thin red laser, then charges: the laser thickens and flickers. In the last
  quarter-second it turns white and stops aiming, which is the moment to step aside. Each hit
  takes half your health.
- In the Inspector, **Range** (30 m by default) sets how far it can see. Keep it short enough
  that it covers only the part of the route you mean it to, and test from the start of the
  chamber.

### Playtests
- Every run in Play mode writes a record to `Logs/Playtests/` (not committed). It holds the
  deaths with where and why, the checkpoints, boots switches, damage, completion, and a coarse
  path.
- **GravBoots → Playtests → Show in Scene View** draws them over the open chamber:
  - a coloured line per run,
  - red dots labelled with the cause where people died,
  - green dots at the checkpoints they reached,
  - a count in the corner.
- **Open Folder** opens the records folder. Delete old records there when you change the chamber
  a lot.

## 4. Testing
1. Press **Play**, then press **E** on a magnetic surface to switch the boots on, and walk.
2. **R** goes back to the last checkpoint, **Backspace** restarts the chamber, and **N** jumps
   to the next spawn or checkpoint. **F3** shows the debug overlay.
3. If something blocks or drops you unexpectedly, check whether a join is sharp (no curve)
   or a surface isn't magnetic.
